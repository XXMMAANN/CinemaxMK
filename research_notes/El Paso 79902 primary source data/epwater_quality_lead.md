## Q1. Detected-contaminant tables, 2023 / 2024 / 2025 data years (CCRs published 2024 / 2025 / 2026)

### Takeaway
None of the three CCRs reports an MCL or action-level exceedance.

- **Arsenic:** the system average was 5.5 ppb (2023), **4.3 ppb (2024)** and 3.4 ppb (2025). The **6.6 ppb** figure does not appear as arsenic in any CCR. It matches the 2024 *lead* maximum (6.6 ppb), so an aggregator probably mixed up the rows.
- **HAA5 (THAA):**
  - Highest LRAA: 34.05 ppb (2023), **35.43 ppb (2024)** and 19.23 ppb (2025).
  - The aggregator's "0.06 mg/L" is the MCL itself, not a result.
- **TTHM highest LRAA:** 70.45, 71.03 and 51.03 ppb.
- **Lead 90th percentile:** 1.5 ppb (2022 sampling), 1.16 ppb (2024) and 0 ppb (2025).
- **PFAS (UCMR 5):** individual detections were near the 2024 federal MCL levels. PFOS reached a maximum of 6.05 ppt in 2023 and 4.27 ppt in 2024; the PFOS MCL is 4.0 ppt. In the 2025 data year, every PFAS result was below the reporting limit.
- **Violations:** the only violations were monitoring/reporting ones:
  - chlorite, May 2023 and March 2024
  - Revised Total Coliform Rule (RTCR) routine monitoring, February 2023

### Inferences
- [INF] Resolution of the conflicting figures from the earlier notes:
  - **Arsenic:** 4.3 ppb is the correct 2024 system average. The 6.6 ppb figure is misattributed; it is the 2024 maximum lead result. The 21 ppb figure is unsupported.
  - **HAA5:** the 2024 highest LRAA was 35.43 ppb, about 59% of the MCL. "0.06 mg/L" is just the MCL.
  - **PFAS:**
    - Some individual 2023 and 2024 samples of PFOS (6.05 and 4.27 ppt) were above the 4.0 ppt PFOS MCL that EPA set in April 2024.
    - PFBS, PFHxS and others were well below their thresholds.
    - MCL compliance is judged on running annual averages at each entry point, and enforcement begins in the future, so these detections are not violations.
    - Every 2025 sample was below the reporting limit.
- [INF] Disinfection byproducts dropped sharply in 2025 (TTHM LRAA from 71 to 51 ppb; HAA5 from 35 to 19 ppb). This fits the very short 2025 Rio Grande season: less organic-rich surface water was being chlorinated. That is consistent with the source mix, but the CCR does not say it.
- [INF] In each year, the maximum lead result (4.3, 6.6 and 12.9 ppb) and the maximum copper result (0.58, 0.81 and 0.687 ppm) were below the action levels. So **0 sampled sites exceeded the lead or copper action level** in 2022, 2024 and 2025. This is derived from the printed ranges, not stated explicitly.
- [INF] The 2025 lead 90th percentile of "0" probably means the 90th-percentile sample was below the reporting limit (<1 ppb). However, the 2025 maximum (12.9 ppb) is about double the 2024 maximum.

## Q3. TCEQ Drinking Water Watch / Drinking Water Viewer (TX0710002)

### Takeaway
The old TCEQ Drinking Water Watch (dww2.tceq.texas.gov) has been **decommissioned** and now redirects users to the new **Drinking Water Viewer** at https://dwv.tceq.texas.gov/.

The DWV is an Angular app. Its data endpoints returned HTTP 500 errors to direct requests; they appear to need a browser session or reCAPTCHA. As a result, I could not pull TCEQ's lead/copper sample summaries, sample-site counts or entry-point data. ## Q4. Which treatment plants and sources serve 79902

### Takeaway
EPWater's own documents point to the **Robertson/Umbenhauer ("Canal Street") Water Treatment Plant**, at 800 Canal St, ZIP 79901, just southeast of 79902, as the plant for **Central and West El Paso**. It treats Rio Grande water from March to September. Off-season, the "Downtown Central" area gets groundwater from the Airport and Lower Valley/Central well fields. The western part of 79902 (Rim, Kern, Mission Hills) may also receive Upper Valley well-field water.

### Cited Findings
- **Robertson/Umbenhauer fact sheet** [PV], October 2017:
  - "Located at 800 Canal Street (79901)"; 40 MGD combined.
  - "treats Rio Grande water to drinking water standards during the irrigation season (March-September)… can also blend and treat water pumped from wells during the non-irrigation season."
  - "Area(s) Served: **Central and West El Paso**."
  - The Robertson plant opened in 1943 and Umbenhauer was added in 1967. A UV disinfection system was installed in 2004.
  - Source: [Robertson/Umbenhauer fact sheet](https://www.epwater.org/ep-water/uploads/robertson-umbenhauer-water-plant.pdf)

### Inferences
- [INF] For 79902 the seasonal pattern is:
  - **March to September** (when river water is available): mainly Canal Street (Robertson/Umbenhauer) treated Rio Grande water. TDS was about 650 mg/L and hardness about 235 mg/L (2017 sheet).
  - **October to February:** mainly well water from the Airport and Lower Valley/Central fields. Source 7 had the highest TDS (862), sodium (217) and chloride (283) of any source.
  - The western part of 79902 may get Upper Valley water year-round.
  - In the 2025 data year, Canal Street's two units had the system's highest turbidity readings (0.38 and 0.36 NTU), and Umbenhauer met the standard in 96% of samples, though there was no violation.
- [INF] Shortened river seasons in drought years mean 79902 spends more of the year on the higher-TDS groundwater blend.

### Gaps
- EPWater publishes no current pressure-zone or ZIP-level source map. The "attached distribution maps" mentioned in chemanalysis.pdf are not included in the PDF.
- The plant-to-area mapping dates from 2017 and may have changed.
