## Q1: Which water system and treatment sources serve 79902?

### Takeaway
ZIP 79902 is in the main City of El Paso system, El Paso Water Utilities, PWS ID **TX0710002**. It serves about 747,000 people from a blend of Rio Grande surface water (treated at the Jonathan Rogers and Robertson/Umbenhauer plants), Hueco Bolson and Mesilla Bolson groundwater, and the Kay Bailey Hutchison desalination plant. I found no public source that maps which plant feeds 79902 specifically.

### Cited Findings
- The EPA PWS ID for El Paso Water is TX0710002. Its primary source is listed as surface water, and it serves 747,168 residents (aggregator/EWG listing). Sources: [EWG Tap Water Database](https://www.ewg.org/tapwater/system.php?pws=TX0710002); [tapwater.org](https://www.tapwater.org/texas/el-paso)
- EPWater has four treatment plants: Kay Bailey Hutchison Desalination Plant, Jonathan Rogers WTP, Robertson/Umbenhauer WTP and Upper Valley WTP. Together they can produce about 220 million gallons per day (MGD) on average. Source: [El Paso Inc, "By the numbers"](https://www.elpasoinc.com/news/local_news/by-the-numbers-where-el-paso-gets-its-water/article_503b196d-eeb3-4e6d-977e-51412db054a4.html) (snippet)
- Supply split: Rio Grande about 44%, Hueco Bolson about 36%, Mesilla Bolson about 20%. Since 2020, the Hueco Bolson has supplied as little as 36% and as much as 61% of the city's water in a given year. Source: [El Paso Inc](https://www.elpasoinc.com/news/local_news/by-the-numbers-where-el-paso-gets-its-water/article_503b196d-eeb3-4e6d-977e-51412db054a4.html); [EPWater aquifers page](https://www.epwater.org/our-water/water-resources/our-aquifers) (snippets)
- The KBH Desalination Plant is a joint project with Fort Bliss that treats brackish Hueco Bolson groundwater. Its capacity is 27.5 MGD, but it currently treats about 8 MGD, with expansion planned to about 33 MGD. Source: [El Paso Matters, Nov 2023](https://elpasomatters.org/2023/11/21/facility-to-boost-el-pasos-water-supply/) (snippet)
- In June 2026, EPWater began its seasonal transition to treating Rio Grande water at two of its plants. Source: [EPWater newsroom](https://www.epwater.org/about-us/newsroom/epwater-to-begin-seasonal-transition-to-rio-grande-water-supply) (snippet)

### Inferences
- The Robertson/Umbenhauer plant is near downtown, and the Jonathan Rogers plant is in the Lower Valley. During the river season (about late spring to August/September), central El Paso (79902: Sunset Heights, Kern Place, Manhattan Heights) likely gets a blend that includes treated Rio Grande water. Off-season it is mostly groundwater. This is an inference; it is not documented.
- Because the blend shifts by season, the chemistry of 79902's water (total dissolved solids, arsenic, disinfection byproducts) likely changes through the year.

### Gaps
- I found no source naming the plant or pressure zone that serves 79902.
- I could not confirm whether any 79902 addresses are served by a different PWS.

## Q2: CCR detected contaminants vs MCLs (2024 report on CY2023, 2025 report on CY2024)

### Takeaway
Secondary sources say EPWater's recent reports show no MCL violations. Arsenic is natural and stays under the 10 ppb limit as a system average, though individual sites reportedly reach 21 ppb. TTHM is relatively high, with an LRAA of 71.03 ppb against an MCL of 80. Fluoride is natural, at about 0.29 ppm on average. The lead 90th percentile is low, about 1.1 ppb. I could not verify exact values against the CCR PDFs.

### Cited Findings
- EPWater has published a 2025 Drinking Water Report, and reports back to 2014 are on its site. Source: [EPWater Drinking Water Report page](https://www.epwater.org/our-water/water-quality/drinking-water-report) (search snippet). The URL pattern for older reports is `https://cdnsm5-hosted.civiclive.com/UserFiles/Servers/Server_6843404/File/Our%20Water/Water%20Quality/dwr_2006.pdf`.
- **Arsenic** (MCL 10 ppb):
  - One aggregator reports 4.3 ppb, or 43% of the MCL. Source: [tapwater.org](https://www.tapwater.org/texas/el-paso)
  - Another summary says the system-wide average is 6.6 ppb, and that levels reached 21 ppb "in areas drawing predominantly from groundwater." Source: snippet from [tapwater.org / epwater search result](https://www.tapwater.org/texas/el-paso)
  - The two figures conflict. A 21 ppb single-site value is plausible because Texas arsenic compliance is based on running annual averages at each entry point.
- **Nitrate** (MCL 10 ppm): 0.92 ppm. Source: [tapwater.org / hydroviv aggregator](https://www.tapwater.org/texas/el-paso) (snippet)
- **Fluoride**:
  - El Paso Inc cites the 2024 EPWater drinking water report: 0.112 to 0.514 ppm across sites, with an average of 0.29 ppm.
  - EPWater does not add fluoride; all of it is naturally occurring. That level is below the 0.7 ppm federal recommendation.
  - Source: [El Paso Inc](https://www.elpasoinc.com/factiva/fluoride-in-el-paso-s-drinking-water-its-all-natural/article_efc4a39e-16f9-417b-978e-4c6e3e87ad1c.html)
- **TTHM** (MCL 80 ppb): the LRAA is 71.03 ppb, or about 89% of the MCL. The aggregator labels this as "2024." Source: [tapwater.org](https://www.tapwater.org/texas/el-paso)
- **HAA5** (MCL 60 ppb): reported as "0.06 mg/l." This equals 60 ppb, the MCL itself, so it may be a misreading by the aggregator (for example, the MCL shown in place of the result). Treat as unverified. Source: aggregator snippet ([tapwaterdata.com](https://www.tapwaterdata.com/tx/el-paso))
- **TDS, sodium, chloride and sulfate**:
  - An aggregator lists maximums of 2,080 ppm TDS, 256 ppm sodium, 220 ppm chloride and 436 ppm sulfate. These likely come from individual wells or sources, not tap averages.
  - Other sources give typical TDS of about 300 to 650 mg/L, depending on source and neighborhood.
  - Secondary standards: TDS 1,000 mg/L under Texas rules (500 under federal guidance), chloride 300, sulfate 300.
  - Source: [aggregator snippets](https://www.tapwaterdata.com/tx/el-paso); EPWater also publishes a chemical analysis sheet: [chemanalysis.pdf](https://cdnsm5-hosted.civiclive.com/UserFiles/Servers/Server_6843404/File/Our%20Water/Water%20Quality/chemanalysis.pdf) (not fetchable)
- **Hardness**: 207 ppm (12.1 grains per gallon), rated "very hard." Source: [tapwater.org](https://www.tapwater.org/texas/el-paso)
- **Lead and copper**:
  - A summary of the 2025-report data gives a lead 90th percentile of 1.1 ppb, against an action level of 15 ppb. Source: [tapwater.org](https://www.tapwater.org/texas/el-paso) (snippet)
  - Another aggregator lists 0.0 ppb with a garbled date ("2027"), so that figure is unreliable. Source: [waterverge](https://www.waterverge.com/cities/el-paso-tx/)
  - Older context: 1991 and 1994 lead surveys found 0.0035 mg/L (3.5 ppb) at the 90th percentile. Source: [2006 DWR](https://cdnsm5-hosted.civiclive.com/UserFiles/Servers/Server_6843404/File/Our%20Water/Water%20Quality/dwr_2006.pdf) (snippet)
- **PFAS (UCMR 5)**:
  - EPWater sampled wells and treated river water from 2023 to early 2025.
  - It reports that PFAS were "detected in a few locations ... at levels that are generally below the proposed regulatory thresholds." One summary says the results are below the 2024 MCLs of 4 ppt for PFOA and PFOS.
  - Source: [EPWater PFAS & Unregulated Contaminants page](https://www.epwater.org/our-water/water-quality/pfas-and-unregulated-contaminants) (snippet)
  - "Generally below" implies some detections may be near or at threshold levels.
- **Lithium (UCMR 5)**: ASU's Paul Westerhoff wrote about small amounts of lithium found in El Paso's drinking water. Source: [ASU Westerhoff blog](https://faculty.engineering.asu.edu/westerhoff/?p=4192) (title only)
- **Compliance**: an aggregator says the 2025 report confirms the system "meets or exceeds" all federal and TCEQ standards. Source: [cleanairandwater.net / aggregator](https://cleanairandwater.net/el-paso-tx/)

### Inferences
- The main chemistry issues for 79902 are natural arsenic, high mineral content and hardness (from groundwater and desalination blending), and disinfection byproducts that run close to the MCL. DBPs tend to be higher when chlorinated Rio Grande water, which is rich in organics, is in the mix during summer.
- The specific UCMR 5 PFAS values are unknown. The utility's wording ("generally below") is hedged.

  - any violations or Tier 3 notices

## Q3: Lead Service Line Inventory (LCRR, due Oct 16, 2024)

### Takeaway
EPWater says it has found **no lead service lines**. Copper is the dominant material. The initial inventory nonetheless has many "unknown" lines that had not yet been inspected. An address-searchable inventory map is on EPWater's website. Customers with lead, galvanized-requiring-replacement or unknown lines were to be notified by December 2025. I could not find exact category totals.

### Cited Findings
- Copper is the most widely used service line material. EPWater "has not located any lead pipes" and says work continues "until we know the material of every last line." Source: [EPWater Lead Awareness page](https://www.epwater.org/our-water/water-quality/lead-awareness); [El Paso Matters opinion, Sept 27, 2024](https://elpasomatters.org/2024/09/27/opinion-el-paso-water-lead-pipe-inspections/) (snippets)
- As of November 2024, an EPWater team was verifying about **140,000 properties**. The utility already had records for about **70,000** more. No lead lines had been found. Source: [KVIA, Nov 26, 2024](https://kvia.com/health/2024/11/26/el-paso-water-investigating-lead-pipes-citywide/) (snippet)
- Crews targeted service lines installed before 1989 (when Texas restricted lead pipes) and lines with no recorded material. They identified material on both the utility and customer sides of the meter. Source: [KFOX14](https://kfoxtv.com/news/local/older-el-paso-homes-may-face-lead-plumbing-risk-ep-water-conducts-thorough-inspections) (snippet)
- "Unknown" means crews had not yet inspected the line. EPWater planned to inspect all unknown lines over the following year, from main to meter and meter to building. Source: [EPWater Lead Awareness page](https://www.epwater.org/our-water/water-quality/lead-awareness) (snippet)
- Notices:
  - Customers with lead, galvanized-requiring-replacement or unknown lines were to receive a notice by December 2025.
  - Notices for unknown lines repeat every year until the material is confirmed.
  - Source: [EPWater Lead Awareness page](https://www.epwater.org/our-water/water-quality/lead-awareness) (snippet)
- An address-searchable lead service line inventory map is on the EPWater website. Source: [EPWater Lead Awareness page](https://www.epwater.org/our-water/water-quality/lead-awareness) (snippet)
- EPA also publishes national service line inventory data. Source: [EPA SDWIS Service Line Inventory](https://sdwis.epa.gov/ords/sfdw_pub/r/sfdw/sdwis_fed_reports_public/service-line-inventory) (blocked); [EPA LSL inventory summary Nov 2025](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-11/lsl-inventory_2025.11.20.pdf)

### Inferences
- 79902 includes some of El Paso's oldest housing: Sunset Heights from the late 1800s to the 1920s, Kern Place from the 1910s to the 1920s, and Manhattan Heights from the 1920s to the 1930s. These pre-1989 areas were probably in the inspection priority set and may have had a high share of "unknown" lines in the initial inventory. However, no source reports neighborhood-level results.
- With no lead lines found, galvanized-requiring-replacement lines would only exist where galvanized pipe sat downstream of lead. Even so, older homes may still have **premise plumbing** with lead solder (before 1986–89) or galvanized interior pipe. The utility inventory does not cover this.

### Gaps
- I found no exact counts of lead, galvanized-requiring-replacement, unknown or non-lead lines. The EPA SDWIS inventory and EPWater pages were blocked.
- I found no data at ZIP or neighborhood level for 79902.
- I found no replacement plan or funding amounts. If zero lead lines are confirmed, a replacement program may not be needed. I found no DWSRF/BIL lead funding to EPWater.
- I could not determine the status of the inventory as of late 2026, such as whether all unknowns were resolved.

## Q4: Notable issues (notices, wastewater, taste/odor, drought)

### Takeaway
The biggest recent controversy is the 2021–22 Frontera force main failure. It sent about 1.1 billion gallons of raw sewage into the Rio Grande, but it affected the river and wastewater, not the drinking water. Drought is cutting Rio Grande supply in 2025–2026, which pushes more reliance on groundwater and desalination. I found no drinking-water MCL violations.

### Cited Findings
- **Frontera force main spill**:
  - Corroded Frontera force mains broke on August 13, 2021, after heavy rain.
  - From August 2021 to January 2022, EPWater diverted more than 1.1 billion gallons of untreated sewage into the Rio Grande, at about 6 to 10 million gallons per day. The diversion was downstream of Sunland Park, NM.
  - Some of the sewage was routed via an irrigation canal to the Roberto Bustamante WWTP.
  - Sources: [El Paso Matters, June 9, 2022](https://elpasomatters.org/2022/06/09/new-mexico-fines-el-paso-water-1-2-million-for-rio-grande-sewage-dump/); [El Paso Matters, Nov 1, 2021](https://elpasomatters.org/2021/11/01/el-paso-water-starts-new-phase-of-river-sewage-cleanup/); [El Paso Matters, Mar 10, 2022](https://elpasomatters.org/2022/03/10/el-paso-water-others-continue-sewage-clean-up-before-rio-grade-runs-in-june/)
- **Penalties for the spill**:
  - The New Mexico Environment Department fined EPWater $1.2 million, which EPWater disputed. Source: [El Paso Matters, Aug 8, 2022](https://elpasomatters.org/2022/08/08/documents-el-paso-water-takes-1-2m-fine-fight-across-state-lines/)
  - EPWater faced about a $2 million TCEQ penalty. It reached an agreement after spending about $7 million on Rio Grande cleanup. Source: [KTSM](https://www.ktsm.com/local/el-paso-news/ep-water-fined-2m-for-frontera-wastewater-dump-into-rio-grande/)
- **Later spills**:
  - A 560,000-gallon spill at the Bustamante plant was contained and cleaned up (date not confirmed in snippet). Source: [CBS4 Local](https://cbs4local.com/newsletter-daily/el-paso-water-contains-560000-gallon-spill-at-bustamante-plant-cleanup-underway)
  - Another wastewater spill near the Rio Grande was reported (date not confirmed). Source: [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/el-paso-water-reports-another-212828142.html)
- **Drought and river supply**:
  - In 2025, El Paso got Rio Grande water only through mid-August. Executives expected a similarly short season in 2026.
  - The EPWater board passed a drought resolution to speed up contracts for well drilling.
  - Elephant Butte was projected to be about 1% full by September 2025.
  - The 2025 irrigation allotment was 6 inches per acre.
  - Sources: [El Paso Matters, June 22, 2025](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/06/22/el-paso-water-resources-rio-grande-drought-elephant-butte/); [El Paso Matters, July 11, 2025](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/07/11/el-paso-news-water-drought-how-to-help-ruidoso-kerrville-flood-utep-faculty-senate/)
  - Earlier context: in August 2023, a drought emergency was issued amid record heat. Source: [El Paso Matters, Aug 7, 2023](https://elpasomatters.org/2023/08/07/drought-emergency-issued-for-el-paso/)
- **Annual demand**: about 40 billion gallons per year. In 2026 the utility expected to rely more on groundwater. Source: [El Paso Matters, June 22, 2025](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/06/22/el-paso-water-resources-rio-grande-drought-elephant-butte/)
- **Advanced purification ("toilet to tap")**: EPWater is moving ahead with direct potable reuse, a new way to stretch its supply. Source: [Texas Tribune, Apr 10, 2025](https://www.texastribune.org/2025/04/10/el-paso-texas-water-crisis/)
- **Other planning**: a 2026 piece covers El Paso's pursuit of a "drought-proof" supply. Source: [Big Bend Sentinel, May 6, 2026](https://bigbendsentinel.com/2026/05/06/our-water-matters-el-paso-pursues-a-drought-proof-water-supply/). Another covers AI and data-center demand on the water plan. Source: [El Paso Inc](https://www.elpasoinc.com/news/local_news/enough-to-go-around-can-el-paso-s-water-plan-handle-thirsty-ai/article_ece67c21-f254-40fb-bfdc-adbb1a825b06.html)

### Inferences
- Shorter river seasons mean more groundwater and desalinated water in the 79902 tap blend for more of the year. That probably means higher TDS, hardness and arsenic, and possibly lower DBPs. This is an inference.
- The Frontera spill was a river and recreation issue. Nothing found links it to tap water quality in 79902.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable information on taste/odor complaints, boil-water notices, or Tier 1/2/3 public notices for TX0710002 in 2023–2026. TCEQ DWW was not reachable.
- I could not confirm the exact dates of the Bustamante 560,000-gallon spill or the later Rio Grande spill.
