## 1. NOAA/NIHHIS 2020 Urban Heat Island Mapping Campaign in El Paso (CAPA Strategies)

### Takeaway
El Paso was one of 13 US cities in NOAA/NIHHIS's 2020 UHI mapping campaign, run with CAPA Strategies, UTEP, the City of El Paso and NWS El Paso. 41 volunteers collected more than 66,000 temperature and humidity readings across about 100–105 sq mi on one extreme-heat day in July 2020, in three one-hour traverses (morning, afternoon, evening). Public summaries say most of the city mapped hot (yellow and red), with cooler pockets near natural landscape. **I found no neighborhood-level temperature values for Kern Place, Sunset Heights, the Rim Road area or downtown, and no citywide morning, afternoon or evening temperature ranges.**

### Cited Findings
- El Paso was one of 13 US cities chosen for NOAA's 2020 UHI Mapping Campaign, a community-science project. — [UTEP SEGA page](https://www.utep.edu/liberalarts/sega/environmental-injustice-hurricane-harvey-in-greater-houston12.html); [NIHHIS Summer 2020 UHI Campaigns](https://nihhis.cpo.noaa.gov/Urban-Heat-Island-Mapping/Summer-2020-UHI-Campaigns); [NOAA CPO, "2020 Urban Heat Island Mapping Campaign Successfully Finishes"](https://cpo.noaa.gov/2020-Urban-Heat-Island-Mapping-Campaign-Successfully-Finishes/)
- NOAA's Climate Program Office supported the campaign, and NIHHIS and CAPA Strategies LLC coordinated it jointly. CAPA provided the sensors, volunteer training, machine-learning analysis and processing to produce the maps. — [NOAA CPO](https://cpo.noaa.gov/2020-Urban-Heat-Island-Mapping-Campaign-Successfully-Finishes/); [CAPA, "City of El Paso maps hottest places in region"](https://www.capastrategies.com/city-of-el-paso-maps-hottest-places-in-region)
- **Partners:** the City of El Paso (now the Office of Climate & Sustainability), UTEP (students from Liberal Arts, Engineering and other programs drove the routes), community-based organizations, and the regional NWS office. The city's page calls the project an "Urban Heat Mapping and Heat Perceptions Study". — [UTEP SEGA](https://www.utep.edu/liberalarts/sega/environmental-injustice-hurricane-harvey-in-greater-houston12.html); [City of El Paso Office of Climate & Sustainability](https://www.elpasotexas.gov/strategic-and-legislative-affairs/climate-action)
- **Traverse date:** UTEP says volunteers collected readings "over three one-hour periods on July 10, 2020". — [UTEP SEGA](https://www.utep.edu/liberalarts/sega/environmental-injustice-hurricane-harvey-in-greater-houston12.html). This conflicts with the CAPA/news summary, which says "Friday's extreme heat" and gives a date of July 11, 2020 in one snippet — [CAPA](https://www.capastrategies.com/city-of-el-paso-maps-hottest-places-in-region). July 10, 2020 was a Friday, so **July 10, 2020 is the more likely date**. The July 11 figure probably reflects the article's publication date.
- **Scale:** 41 volunteers collected more than 66,000 temperature and humidity measurements over 105 sq mi within city limits. — [City of El Paso Climate & Sustainability](https://www.elpasotexas.gov/strategic-and-legislative-affairs/climate-action); [CAPA](https://www.capastrategies.com/city-of-el-paso-maps-hottest-places-in-region). Another summary says El Paso chose "100 square miles", focusing on the most vulnerable areas using UTEP input and census data. — [CAPA / NIHHIS summary via search](https://www.capastrategies.com/city-of-el-paso-maps-hottest-places-in-region)
- **Method:** small thermal sensors were mounted on volunteers' cars and bikes. NWS El Paso helped choose the collection day. CAPA Heat Watch delivers 9 maps and datasets per city: 3 traverse-point maps (morning, afternoon, evening), plus continuous surface maps of temperature and heat index for each period. — [CAPA](https://www.capastrategies.com/city-of-el-paso-maps-hottest-places-in-region)
- **Spatial pattern (qualitative):** "the majority of the heat map is in yellow and red meaning hot … areas closer to natural landscapes are cooler." Cited examples of cooler areas are "large swaths of the far east side, particularly the upper east side" and parts of the Lower Valley with more natural landscape. — [KTSM, "Hottest areas of El Paso…"](https://www.ktsm.com/news/hottest-areas-of-el-paso-how-water-company-prepares-for-drought-conditions/) (city official quoted; publication date not captured)
- Context: El Paso has "one of the lowest tree equity scores in the state of Texas and … the nation", and the UHI effect is a major contributor to the city's rapid warming. — [CBS4 "Evan Explains"](https://cbs4local.com/news/evan-explains/evan-explains-why-is-el-paso-the-uss-third-fastest-warming-city-albedo-drought-urban-heat-island)
- 2020 itself was El Paso's second-hottest year on record (since 1887), with 56 days of 100°F or more, the second-highest count at that time. — [El Paso Matters, 2020-12-31](https://elpasomatters.org/2020/12/31/2020-was-el-pasos-second-hottest-year-on-record/)
- A separate El Paso–Ciudad Juárez urban heat vulnerability assessment exists. It may contain neighborhood-level detail, but I could not open it. — [urban-comfort.eu PDF](https://urban-comfort.eu/reports/el-paso-juarez-research.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Applying this to 79902 (Kern Place, Sunset Heights, Rim Road, UTEP, edge of downtown):** this is inference, not a reported finding. Heat Watch campaigns in other cities consistently find the afternoon and evening hot spots in dense, paved, low-canopy cores such as downtown, and cooler readings near open natural land. 79902 straddles both:
  - Its southern and eastern edges (Sunset Heights, near downtown and I-10) plausibly sit in the hotter urban-core band.
  - Its upslope Franklin Mountains foothill areas (Rim Road, upper Kern Place, near open desert parkland) are likely relatively cooler, especially in the evening because of elevation and mountain drainage air.

  This needs confirmation from the CAPA El Paso final report or the map layers.
- A citywide temperature spread of a few to about 10°F across a city is typical of CAPA campaigns. I found **no El Paso-specific number**, so the writer should not quote one.

### Gaps
- **Not found:** morning, afternoon and evening min–max temperatures, the hottest and coolest specific readings, and neighborhood values for 79902. The CAPA El Paso final report PDF and heat.gov campaign page could not be retrieved because the proxy blocked them.
- The "100 vs 105 sq mi" study area and "July 10 vs 11" date discrepancies are unresolved without the primary report.
- I did not find whether the 2020 maps are posted as GIS layers on the city or heat.gov portals.

## 2. NWS El Paso (EPZ, Santa Teresa NM) heat products: criteria, issuance history, records and 100°F days

### Takeaway
NWS El Paso's Heat Advisory has loosely meant highs of 105°F or more with overnight lows of 75°F or more. Since 2025, warnings are tied to NWS HeatRisk "Major/Extreme" levels and are named "Extreme Heat Watch/Warning" (formerly "Excessive Heat"). El Paso International Airport set the all-time high of 114°F on June 30, 1994. It recorded 70 days of 100°F or more and a 44-day 100°F streak in 2023, 55 days in 2024, and 37 days in 2025. Summer 2026 set a record 108-day streak of 90°F or more, and 2026 is tracking as the hottest year on record. **I could not produce exact advisory and warning counts per year, because the IEM VTEC archive was blocked.**

### Cited Findings
**Criteria**
- NWS El Paso's heat advisory criteria are "loosely based on an afternoon high temperature of 105 or higher, and an overnight low temperature that stays 75 or higher", the idea being that people get too little relief overnight. — [AOL/El Paso Times, "Major heat risk triggers heat advisory in El Paso"](https://www.aol.com/articles/major-heat-risk-triggers-heat-141114000.html) (secondary; quotes NWS)
- Nationally, NWS issues an Extreme Heat Watch or Warning when "Major" (red, level 3) or "Extreme" (magenta, level 4) HeatRisk is forecast. Extreme Heat Warnings go out 12–24 hours ahead. The Heat Advisory name was kept, and "Excessive Heat" products were renamed "Extreme Heat". — [NOAA Extreme heat resource guide](https://www.noaa.gov/media-advisory/extreme-heat-resource-guide); [NWS HeatRisk (WPC)](https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/heatrisk/); [NWS HeatRisk (WRH)](https://www.wrh.noaa.gov/wrh/heatrisk/)
- NWS uses lower thresholds for areas that are not usually as hot, from parts of the TX/OK panhandles westward into New Mexico. — [Fox Weather explainer](https://www.foxweather.com/learn/how-weather-you-are-accustomed-affects-national-weather-service-heat-warnings-advisories)

**Issuance examples** (not a complete count)
- 2024: heat advisories on June 5–6 and June 24–26, 2024. — [El Paso Matters, 2025-01-01](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/01/01/el-paso-texas-record-temperatures-heat-2024-weather/) and related coverage
- 2023: El Paso was under an "excessive heat warning" in August 2023 as records fell and power outages hit. — [Yahoo/El Paso Times](https://news.yahoo.com/excessive-heat-warning-place-el-135036007.html)
- 2026: a heat advisory was in effect June 22–24, 2026, with highs of 102–107°F. Highs of 105–108°F followed in late July. — [KTSM, "Heat advisory issued for El Paso…"](https://www.ktsm.com/news/heat-advisory-issued-for-el-paso-as-temperatures-soar-to-over-100-degrees/); [KTSM nine-day forecast](https://www.ktsm.com/nine-day-forecast/dangerous-heat-continues-through-friday-before-storms-return-this-weekend-in-el-paso/)

**Records at El Paso International Airport**
- The all-time record high is 114°F on June 30, 1994. — [NWS EPZ Extreme Weather Records](https://www.weather.gov/epz/elpaso_extreme_weather); [Yahoo/El Paso Times, Trish Long](https://news.yahoo.com/news/el-pasos-hottest-day-114-185436459.html)
- **2023:** hottest year on record at the time.
  - 70 days of 100°F or more, the annual record.
  - 44 consecutive days of 100°F or more, June 16 – July 29, 2023, beating the 23-day record from 1994. Highs averaged above 105°F during the streak.
  - July 2023 was the hottest month on record. The streak peaked at 111°F on July 19, and the year's peak was 112°F on August 6, 2023.
  - 35 daily record highs.

  Sources: [NWS EPZ 100-degree FAQ](https://weather.gov/epz/elpaso_100_degree_page); [El Paso Matters, 2023-12-29](https://elpasomatters.org/2023/12/29/el-paso-weather-record-heat-2023-climate-change/); [El Paso Matters, 2023-07-30](https://elpasomatters.org/2023/07/30/july-2023-is-hottest-month-ever-in-el-paso/); [KFOX14](https://kfoxtv.com/news/local/el-paso-clocks-multiple-climate-record-highs-in-2023-says-national-weather-service-national-centers-for-envrionmental-information-global-warming-climate-change-texas)
- **2024:** warmest year on record.
  - 55 days of 100°F or more. One source ranks this 4th all-time and another 2nd, so the ranks conflict.
  - 22 daily record highs and two monthly record highs.
  - Warmest June and warmest October on record.
  - Latest 100°F day ever, on September 27, 2024.
  - Average temperature was **69.9°F per El Paso Matters**, or **69.8°F per the NWS 2024 Annual Review** (prefer the NWS figure).

  Sources: [El Paso Matters, 2025-01-01](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/01/01/el-paso-texas-record-temperatures-heat-2024-weather/); [NWS EPZ 2024 Annual Review PDF](https://www.weather.gov/media/epz/climat/2024AnnualReview.pdf); [KVIA, 2024-09-28](https://kvia.com/weather/2024/09/28/el-paso-breaks-record-with-latest-100-degree-day-more-warm-and-dry-weather-ahead/). The rank conflict is visible by comparing El Paso Matters 2020 (56 days in 2020 = 2nd at the time) with the 2024 search summaries.
- **2025:** third-warmest year.
  - 37 days of 100°F or more (11th most), with 33 of them between June 1 and August 31.
  - Peak of 109°F on June 15, during an 11-day 100°F streak.
  - 29 daily record highs set or tied.

  Sources: [El Paso Matters, 2026-01-01](https://elpasomatters.org/2026/01/01/el-paso-third-warmest-year-2025-climate-change/); [El Paso Matters, 2025-09-02](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/09/02/how-hot-was-el-pasos-summer-2025-weather/)
- **2026 (through September 21):**
  - 108 consecutive days of 90°F or more, the longest streak on record, with 50 of those days at 100°F or more.
  - Every day from June 22 to September 21 reached 90°F or more, a first in more than 140 years of records.
  - The June–August average of 86.7°F was the 5th warmest.
  - 2026 was running 0.4°F warmer than any previous year to date.

  Sources: [El Paso Matters, 2026-09-21](https://elpasomatters.org/2026/09/21/el-paso-record-108-consecutive-days-90-degree-heat/); [El Paso Matters, 2026-08-30](https://elpasomatters.org/2026/08/30/el-paso-climate-change-summer-heat-electric-bills/)

### Inferences
- 79902 is in the same NWS public forecast zone as the city of El Paso, and the airport (ELP) is about 5–6 mi to the east-northeast. Airport heat products and statistics therefore apply directly to 79902. Lower-elevation parts of 79902 near downtown probably run as hot as or hotter than the airport. Foothill areas such as Rim Road may run slightly cooler.
- Given the 105/75 rule of thumb, years with 55–70 days of 100°F or more (2023, 2024) almost certainly produced many more advisory or warning days than 2025. I have not verified this with counts.

- The official NWS EPZ local criteria page could not be read. The 105/75 threshold is a media paraphrase of NWS.
- The 2026 annual total of 100°F days and its final rank are not yet available, since the year is in progress.

## 3. Heat-related deaths in El Paso County (2023, 2024, 2025)

### Takeaway
State (DSHS) data reported by El Paso Matters shows record heat deaths: 26 in 2023 and 39 in 2024, counting deaths directly or indirectly caused by heat. 2025 deaths were reported as fewer than the prior two years as of August 2025, but I found no confirmed final 2025 total. Neither the city nor the county publishes a regular heat surveillance report, which is a key gap.

### Cited Findings
- 39 deaths were attributed directly or indirectly to heat in El Paso County in 2024, a record. The prior record was 26 in 2023, "according to state data". — [El Paso Matters, 2025-08-21, "Heat crisis: El Paso deaths soar to record levels"](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/08/21/el-paso-weather-extreme-heat-illness-deaths/)
- Inside Climate News, in a separate analysis of UMC, DSHS, Medical Examiner and fire department records, identified 26 people who died in the heat across 2023 and 2024 combined: 19 migrants and 7 US residents. This counting method differs from El Paso Matters' 26 + 39 and should not be read as contradicting it outright. — [Inside Climate News, 2025-08-17](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/17082025/el-paso-extreme-heat-illness-death/); [Public Health Watch, 2025-08-17](https://publichealthwatch.org/2025/08/17/el-paso-heat-climate-migrants/)
- 2025 so far had "fewer heat deaths reported … than in the previous two years". The first 2025 heat death was an 85-year-old woman in early June. — [El Paso Matters, 2025-08-21](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/08/21/el-paso-weather-extreme-heat-illness-deaths/); [Inside Climate News](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/17082025/el-paso-extreme-heat-illness-death/)
- Neither the city nor the county shares the state's weekly heat-illness data with the public. El Paso has no staff positions dedicated to heat mitigation, unlike Phoenix and Miami-Dade. The El Paso Sector was the deadliest US–Mexico border sector in 2024. — [El Paso Matters, 2025-08-21](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/08/21/el-paso-weather-extreme-heat-illness-deaths/)
- The Medical Examiner's Office declined interviews. Chief ME Dr. Mario Rascon answered questions in writing. — [Inside Climate News](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/17082025/el-paso-extreme-heat-illness-death/)
- An El Paso Matters article on the ME's 2025 annual report exists (2026-06-21), but the search snippet did not give a heat-death figure. — [El Paso Matters, 2026-06-21](https://elpasomatters.org/2026/06/21/deaths-el-paso-county-medical-examiner-report-overdoses-car-accidents/)
- Texas is likely undercounting heat deaths statewide. — [Texas Tribune, 2024-08-14](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/08/14/texas-heat-deaths-undercount/)

### Inferences
- Many El Paso heat deaths involve migrants crossing desert or border terrain and outdoor or unhoused people, not residential exposure. For a central neighborhood like 79902, the most relevant risk groups are elderly residents without adequate AC, outdoor workers, unhoused people near downtown, and hikers in the Franklin Mountains just north of the ZIP.

### Gaps
- No final 2025 or partial 2026 heat-death totals from DSHS, the ME or the health department were found.
- No City of El Paso Department of Public Health heat surveillance report was found; reporting indicates none is published regularly.
- No ZIP-level (79902) heat-illness or death data was found.
- The El Paso Matters (26/39) and Inside Climate News (26 over two years) methods differ and could not be reconciled without the full articles.
