# Blocked primary data leaves 79902's risk unscored

The core finding is negative: **none of the ZIP-level or tract-level index values you asked for could be retrieved from their primary sources.** The research environment's network policy blocked census.gov, cdc.gov/atsdr.cdc.gov, fema.gov, epa.gov, epwater.org, tceq.texas.gov, weather.gov, the Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM) and arcgis.com. As a result, the CDC Heat & Health Index (HHI) percentile for ZCTA 79902, the FEMA National Risk Index (NRI) heat-wave ratings for its census tracts, the official list of those tracts, tract-level American Community Survey (ACS) data, NOAA's neighborhood heat-map temperatures, counts of NWS heat products, and El Paso Water's (EPWater's) 2024/2025 water-quality report tables are **all unobtained**. What does survive, mostly from search-result snippets and news coverage, still shows a consistent picture. 79902 (UTEP, Kern Place, Sunset Heights, Mission Hills, Rim Road, the western edge of downtown) is an older, partly low-income, relatively elderly core of a city that set heat records in 2023, 2024 and 2026, and that recorded **39 heat-related deaths countywide in 2024**. Its tap water comes from El Paso Water (PWS TX0710002). Secondary sources describe that water as compliant with health-based limits, but naturally high in arsenic and minerals and running close to the federal limit for disinfection byproducts. The utility reports **no lead service lines found**, with an unresolved backlog of "unknown" lines. No secondary source reports a recent health-based drinking-water violation, but EPA ECHO's own compliance fields were never seen. Every number below carries an explicit verification label, and the last section lists exactly how to fill each gap.

**How to read the verification labels.** No figure in this report was read directly from a primary dataset or document; nothing reached the "primary-verified" standard. Each figure instead carries one of three labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PS** (primary snippet) | Text from a search-engine snippet of a primary page (CDC, FEMA, NWS, EPWater, TCEQ, NMED), not read in full |
| **SN** (secondary news) | Reported by journalism such as El Paso Matters, KTSM, KVIA or Inside Climate News, usually citing an agency |
| **AG** (aggregator) | Third-party compilations (tapwater.org, Census Reporter, Simplemaps, DisasterAware and similar). Lowest confidence; must be checked against the primary source |

## The two federal heat indices have no retrieved value for 79902

**CDC Heat & Health Index.** The HHI ranks every U.S. ZCTA by national percentile across **25 indicators in 4 modules**: historical heat and health burden, sensitivity, sociodemographic factors, and natural and built environment ([HHI 2024 Technical Documentation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/media/pdfs/2024/07/HHI-2024-Release-Technical-Documentation-508.pdf), PS). The current download is "HHI Data 2024 United States" plus a 2024 data dictionary. The HHI page was last updated **September 4, 2025** ([CDC/ATSDR HHI page](https://atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/php/hhi/index.html), PS). No snippet or secondary source reported 79902's overall or module percentiles. **The value is unobtained, and no estimate is offered.** There is also a definitional conflict. The research brief described an extreme heat day as the 90th percentile of local temperatures, but the documentation snippet says a day above the **95th percentile** of the ZCTA's own 1991–2020 record. The full PDF should settle this. Whether a release newer than the 2024 data existed by October 2026 is also unverified.

**FEMA National Risk Index.** The current version is **v1.20 (December 2025)**. It extends the heat-wave record to November 12, 2024, and replaces CDC's Social Vulnerability Index (SVI) with Census Community Resilience Estimates as its social-vulnerability input. That change makes v1.20 ratings **not comparable with older ratings** ([NRI version documentation](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_national-risk-index_data-version-update-documentation.pdf), PS). On December 15, 2025, FEMA retired the standalone NRI map viewer and moved the data into its Resilience Analysis and Planning Tool, RAPT ([FEMA NRI page](https://www.fema.gov/flood-maps/products-tools/national-risk-index), PS). **No tract-level heat-wave score, rating, expected annual loss or frequency was obtained for any 79902 tract.** The only county-level figures come from older, version-ambiguous sources, and they should not be treated as v1.20 values. ValleyCentral, citing FEMA, gave El Paso County an overall score of **35.67**, rated "relatively high" and 8th highest in Texas ([ValleyCentral](https://www.valleycentral.com/news/local-news/these-texas-counties-face-greatest-risk-of-disaster-fema-indicates/), SN, undated and probably earlier than v1.20). DisasterAware rated county heat risk "Relatively High", with about **$14M** in expected annual loss and social vulnerability "Very High". That last rating probably reflects the SVI-based method that v1.20 dropped ([DisasterAware](https://explore.disasteraware.com/risks/el-paso-tx/), AG). Proprietary scores such as ClimateRiskCheck's 9.9/10 "extreme heat" are not NRI values, and the source quoted two different sets of Texas and U.S. benchmarks ([ClimateRiskCheck](https://www.climateriskcheck.com/risk/texas/el-paso), AG, low reliability).

## NOAA's 2020 heat map exists, but its 79902 temperatures were not retrieved

El Paso was one of **13 cities** in NOAA's 2020 Urban Heat Island Mapping Campaign, run with CAPA Strategies, UTEP, the City of El Paso and NWS El Paso ([NOAA CPO](https://cpo.noaa.gov/2020-Urban-Heat-Island-Mapping-Campaign-Successfully-Finishes/), PS). **41 volunteers collected more than 66,000 temperature and humidity readings** across three one-hour traverses (morning, afternoon, evening) ([City of El Paso Climate & Sustainability](https://www.elpasotexas.gov/strategic-and-legislative-affairs/climate-action), PS). Two details conflict. The traverse date is **July 10, 2020** per [UTEP](https://www.utep.edu/liberalarts/sega/environmental-injustice-hurricane-harvey-in-greater-houston12.html) but July 11 in a [CAPA](https://www.capastrategies.com/city-of-el-paso-maps-hottest-places-in-region) snippet. July 10 was the Friday described, so it is the more likely date. The coverage area is given as both 105 and 100 square miles. The only spatial finding that surfaced is qualitative: "the majority of the heat map is in yellow and red", with cooler readings near natural landscape on the far east side and in parts of the Lower Valley ([KTSM](https://www.ktsm.com/news/hottest-areas-of-el-paso-how-water-company-prepares-for-drought-conditions/), SN). **No temperatures for Kern Place, Sunset Heights, Rim Road or downtown, and no citywide temperature ranges, were found.** One plausible expectation follows from the terrain, but it has not been tested against the maps: the low, paved, downtown-adjacent south of 79902 likely runs hotter than its Franklin Mountains foothill edge.

## NWS records show escalating heat; advisory counts are missing

NWS El Paso's Heat Advisory criteria are "loosely based on an afternoon high temperature of 105 or higher, and an overnight low temperature that stays 75 or higher" ([AOL/El Paso Times quoting NWS](https://www.aol.com/articles/major-heat-risk-triggers-heat-141114000.html), SN). This is a media paraphrase; the official local criteria page was blocked. Nationally, warnings are now tied to "Major" and "Extreme" levels of the NWS HeatRisk product, and "Excessive Heat" products were renamed "Extreme Heat" ([NOAA](https://www.noaa.gov/media-advisory/extreme-heat-resource-guide), PS). **Exact yearly counts of heat advisories and warnings for El Paso County zones in 2023–2026 were not obtained.** Only individual events surfaced: advisories on June 5–6 and June 24–26, 2024 ([El Paso Matters](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/01/01/el-paso-texas-record-temperatures-heat-2024-weather/), SN); an excessive heat warning in August 2023 ([Yahoo/El Paso Times](https://news.yahoo.com/excessive-heat-warning-place-el-135036007.html), SN); and an advisory on June 22–24, 2026 ([KTSM](https://www.ktsm.com/news/heat-advisory-issued-for-el-paso-as-temperatures-soar-to-over-100-degrees/), SN).

The temperature record at El Paso International Airport, about 5–6 miles from 79902, is better documented. The all-time high is **114°F on June 30, 1994** ([NWS EPZ records](https://www.weather.gov/epz/elpaso_extreme_weather), PS). Recent years:

The human toll is rising. State data reported by El Paso Matters counts **26 heat-related deaths in El Paso County in 2023 and 39 in 2024**, each a record ([El Paso Matters](https://elpasomatters.org/2025/08/21/el-paso-weather-extreme-heat-illness-deaths/), SN). Inside Climate News, working from different records, identified **26 deaths across 2023 and 2024 combined**, 19 of them migrants ([Inside Climate News](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/17082025/el-paso-extreme-heat-illness-death/), SN). These counts measure different things and cannot be reconciled from snippets. No final 2025 total and no ZIP-level heat-illness data were found. Neither the city nor the county publishes the state's weekly heat-illness data.

## El Paso Water: compliant by secondary accounts, unverified against its own reports

ZIP 79902 is served by El Paso Water Utilities, **PWS TX0710002**, which blends Rio Grande surface water, Hueco and Mesilla Bolson groundwater, and desalinated water from the Kay Bailey Hutchison plant ([EWG](https://www.ewg.org/tapwater/system.php?pws=TX0710002), AG; [El Paso Inc](https://www.elpasoinc.com/news/local_news/by-the-numbers-where-el-paso-gets-its-water/article_503b196d-eeb3-4e6d-977e-51412db054a4.html), SN). The population served conflicts: **747,168** per EWG versus **672,538** per mytapwater.org (both AG). No source maps which treatment plant feeds 79902. EPWater has published a 2025 Drinking Water Report ([EPWater](https://www.epwater.org/our-water/water-quality/drinking-water-report), PS), but **neither the 2024 nor the 2025 report could be opened.** The figures below therefore come from snippets, mostly aggregator snippets.

The copper 90th percentile, radionuclides, bromate, chlorite and the number of sites above the lead action level were all unobtained.

**Lead service line inventory.** EPWater says it "has not located any lead pipes" and that copper is the dominant material ([EPWater Lead Awareness](https://www.epwater.org/our-water/water-quality/lead-awareness), PS). In November 2024 it was field-verifying about **140,000 properties** and already had records for about **70,000** more ([KVIA](https://kvia.com/health/2024/11/26/el-paso-water-investigating-lead-pipes-citywide/), SN). Crews prioritized lines installed before 1989 and lines with no recorded material ([KFOX14](https://kfoxtv.com/news/local/older-el-paso-homes-may-face-lead-plumbing-risk-ep-water-conducts-thorough-inspections), SN). Customers with "unknown" lines were to be notified by December 2025, with repeat notices each year ([EPWater](https://www.epwater.org/our-water/water-quality/lead-awareness), PS). **No category counts were obtained**: lead, galvanized requiring replacement, unknown and non-lead are all missing. No ZIP-level or neighborhood-level breakdown was found either. This gap matters for 79902 in particular. Its pre-1930 housing stock in Sunset Heights and Kern Place is exactly what the "unknown" category would capture, and the utility inventory does not cover lead solder or galvanized pipe inside homes.

**EPA ECHO and enforcement.** ECHO's own fields were never retrieved: compliance status, Serious Violator flag and quarters in noncompliance. Aggregators conflict sharply. One reports **12 violations in the past 3 years, all monitoring or reporting, none health-based**, and 69 on record ([tapwater.org](https://www.tapwater.org/texas/el-paso), AG). Another reports **858 on record, 192 of them health-based** ([mytapwater.org](https://mytapwater.org/pws/tx0710002/el-paso-water-utilities-public-service-b/el-paso-tx/), AG). The second figure probably counts records differently, but it is unusable until checked against EPA's Safe Drinking Water Information System (SDWIS). The documented enforcement is mostly about wastewater, not tap water. The Frontera force main failure diverted about **1.2 billion gallons** of raw sewage to the Rio Grande between August 2021 and January 2022. TCEQ's Agreed Order (Docket 2022-0310-MWD-E) set a **$2,016,000** penalty, offset by a Supplemental Environmental Project ([TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/agency/decisions/agendas/backup/2022/2022-0310-mwd-e.pdf), PS; [KTSM](https://www.ktsm.com/local/el-paso-news/ep-water-fined-2m-for-frontera-wastewater-dump-into-rio-grande/), SN). The volume itself conflicts: El Paso Matters reports more than 1.1 billion gallons, TCEQ about 1.2 billion. New Mexico's **$1.2M** fine was later withdrawn ([Source NM](https://sourcenm.com/briefs/nm-officials-withdraw-1-2-million-fine-against-el-paso-water/), SN). The only drinking-water penalty found is a **$7,728** TCEQ fine in 2022 for the utility's water-hauling facility ([El Paso Matters](https://elpasomatters.org/2022/07/14/tceq-fines-el-paso-utility-for-drinking-water-truck-health-safety-violations/), SN). The former ASARCO smelter, often linked to the area, is at **2301 W. Paisano Dr, ZIP 79922, not 79902** ([TCEQ ASARCO](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/remediation/sites/asarco), PS). Its lead and arsenic legacy did reach Kern Place soils, which were part of EPA's residential cleanup ([EPA El Paso County Metals](https://response.epa.gov/elpasocountymetals), PS). No ECHO facility list for 79902 itself was obtained.

## Conclusion

The evidence that did surface points in one direction: 79902 combines a documented regional heat crisis with a population profile (poverty near 22.5%, median age around 40, very old housing) associated with heat vulnerability. Its water risks, as reported, are chronic and natural (arsenic, minerals, byproducts near their limit) rather than acute violations. That is a plausible hypothesis, not a measured result. The figures that would turn it into a ranking (HHI percentile, NRI tract ratings, tract-level ACS data) are precisely the ones the blocked hosts hold. Any comparative claim, such as "79902 ranks in the top decile for heat risk", must wait for rows 1–3 of the table above.

One structural point should shape the next round of work. The tract list in row 1 is the dependency for rows 3, 5 and 6. It should be retrieved first, and tract-level results should be reported separately rather than averaged. 79902 spans hillside Rim Road and dense Sunset Heights, and a ZIP-wide figure will hide exactly the pockets where heat exposure, old plumbing and low income overlap.
